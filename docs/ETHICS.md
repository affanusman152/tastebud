# Study, don't copy

tastebud can check any public web page. That makes it a good way to learn from great design. It also raises the question of where learning ends and copying begins.

## Fine

- Checking any public page to learn how it's built: its type scale, spacing system, colors and accessibility.
- Writing down what you learned and the reasons behind it ("they use a 5px spacing base", "the heading is 3× the body size").
- Using those **ideas** in your own work, written in your own code with your own content.
- Checking your own work, a client's work with their permission, or a school project.
- Sharing a report about a public site to discuss it, for example in class.

## Not fine

- Copying someone's code, images, illustrations, icons, logos or text into your project.
- Making a page that looks like it belongs to another company (their name, branding or look-alike design).
- Using a report to mock or shame a person's work in public. Critique the design, not the designer.
- Running tastebud over and over against a site you don't own in a way that loads its servers (for example, in a loop).

## Why it matters

Ideas, like "use one accent color for actions" or "give the main heading room", aren't owned by anyone. Specific code, images and words usually are. And copying someone else's look teaches you less than understanding why it works.

The best use of tastebud on someone else's site is to answer one question: **what decision did they make here, and why?** Then make your own decision for your own project.

## A note on scores of famous sites

Well-known sites sometimes score low on Access. That doesn't mean their designers are bad. Big sites have many teams, old code and trade-offs we can't see. Use it to learn, not to dunk.
